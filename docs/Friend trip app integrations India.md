# Links beat licences for Indian friend-trip apps

As of October 2026, a small Indian consumer app can run a whole friend-group villa trip on free links that need no credentials: UPI pay links to the organiser, WhatsApp `wa.me` shares, Google Maps URLs, `.ics` calendar files and Open-Meteo forecasts. Almost every feature that sounds more powerful is locked. Pooling friends' money needs an RBI payment-aggregator licence. There is no official API for posting into a user's existing WhatsApp group or creating a shared Google Photos album. Airbnb offers neither an API nor an affiliate programme, and IRCTC sells only through licensed partners. Three payment rules shape the core mechanic. NPCI ended P2P collect requests on 1 October 2025. UPI's "hold" product (Reserve Pay) is for merchants only and capped at ₹10,000. Handling other people's funds needs ₹15 crore of net worth. So "money moves only if enough friends commit" has to be a pledge that involves no money; once the threshold is met, each friend pays the organiser by UPI. Branded Indian villas refund in full until 14 days before check-in, refund 50% until 7 days, and refund nothing after that. Airbnb's Limited and Firm policies have 14-day and 30-day cut-offs, and Airbnb makes anyone paying in rupees pay in full upfront. The pledge deadline should therefore close about 17–21 days before check-in, which leaves time to collect money before the free-cancellation cut-off. Booking revenue is thin. Booking.com's Awin programme and Agoda's affiliate API are the only open, live options. Airbnb pays no cash, and the MakeMyTrip, ixigo and redBus programmes were paused when checked. Payment-gateway fees of about 2% plus GST, once the launch offers end, are a cost to whoever acts as merchant. They are not income for an app that stays out of the money flow.

## Six integrations need no credentials; the most valuable ones need partners

Ranking every integration against the five-class scheme points one way. The tools that help friends coordinate are free and open. The tools that touch money, inventory or other people's group spaces are regulated, need a partner, or no longer exist. Six integrations work today with no credentials at all: the `upi://pay` link, WhatsApp click-to-chat, `.ics` files, Google's "add to calendar" template link (which is undocumented), Google Maps URLs, and Open-Meteo for as long as the app is non-commercial. Everything else needs keys, a partner, or a person copying something by hand. The table below is the core answer. The sections after it explain the entries that matter most.

Integrations in classes 1 and 4 together cover every step a friend trip needs: deciding, paying the organiser, booking, getting there, staying in touch, sharing photos and settling up. The things that need a partner (classes 2 and 3) add automation and revenue. None of them is needed to run a working MVP. The rest of this report explains what drives each classification, starting with money, because payment rules constrain the product's central promise more than anything else.

## Since October 2025, every rupee between friends must be pushed by the payer

The decisive rule is NPCI's circular of 29 July 2025. It directed banks, payment apps and UPI apps to ensure that **"no P2P collect transaction is initiated, routed, or processed on UPI beyond October 1, 2025"** ([PTI via The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/14/dcm65-biz-npci-collect.html)). Before the ban, P2P collect requests were already capped at ₹2,000. NPCI withdrew them because of fraud. Collect requests from merchants still work ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/personal-finance/banking/story/npci-to-tighten-upi-rules-peer-to-peer-collect-feature-to-end-in-october-489244-2025-08-13)). An app can therefore no longer make a friend's UPI app show "pay ₹12,000 to Priya". It can only notify the friend and give them a link or QR code, and the friend starts the payment.

That link is officially defined. NPCI's deep-linking specification (v1.6) lays out `upi://pay?pa=…&pn=…&am=…&tn=…&cu=INR`, with the payee VPA, name, fixed amount and a short note. When a link has no signature, the payer's app must **"warn the user that the source could not be verified and require a passcode"**. The spec does not tell apps to reject unsigned links. It also tells merchants to confirm payment status on their own server rather than trust the app's response ([NPCI spec v1.6](https://www.labnol.org/files/linking.pdf)). A trip app has no server-side view of a transfer between two friends, so confirmation has to be manual: the organiser marks the payment received, or the payer pastes the UTR (the transaction reference). P2P transfers stay free whatever the amount, even under the merchant-fee proposal ([Khaleej Times / ANI](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/india-defers-upi-charges-on-some-merchant-transactions-to-after-diwali)). The P2P limit stays at **₹1 lakh per day** ([News On AIR](https://newsonair.gov.in/npci-hikes-upi-limit-for-merchant-payments-to-%e2%82%b910-lakh-per-day/)). One untested risk needs a pilot check. A 2020 forum post says Google Pay and PhonePe capped payments opened from deep links at ₹4,000 per transaction ([Team-BHP](https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/shifting-gears/224528-advice-payment-gateways-3.html)). That claim is unverified and old, but villa shares regularly run above ₹10,000, so the app should also show a QR code and a copyable VPA as fallbacks.

### Why the app cannot hold the pot

The obvious design is for friends to pay the app and for the app to release the money once enough friends have joined. That design runs into RBI's Master Direction on Payment Aggregators of 15 September 2025. Any non-bank that aggregates customer payments and handles the funds needs RBI authorisation. It must be a company with **₹15 crore net worth at application, rising to ₹25 crore by the third year**, and it must keep an escrow account at a scheduled commercial bank that is used only for aggregator business. Only pure technology gateways that never touch funds fall outside the rules ([RBI Master Direction](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896)). The new rules also reach the payment gateways themselves. Razorpay's split-settlement product, Route, now requires a minimum turnover and linked accounts that "directly interface with your customers to provide goods or services". Accounts without proof had Route switched off after 31 December 2025 ([Razorpay Route docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/)). The only pooled-money product found in India, Coupl, runs on a licensed issuer's rails ("Issued by LivQuik Technology… RBI-authorized PPI issuer") ([Coupl](https://coupl.money/bill-split-app)).

### Why UPI's "hold" feature does not fit yet

On paper, UPI already has the right feature. In 2022 RBI introduced "single-block-and-multiple-debits", under which funds stay blocked in the payer's own account until they are debited. RBI named **hotel bookings** as an intended use ([RBI, 7 Dec 2022](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=54819)). The product that reached the market, UPI Reserve Pay, falls short in four ways. Razorpay offers it with a **maximum block of ₹10,000** and up to 90 days' validity, only for eligible business categories, and PhonePe supports it on credit cards and credit lines only ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/recurring-payments/upi-reserve-pay.md)). Pine Labs lists the eligible merchant category codes as groceries, food, taxis, transport and similar. The list does not include lodging. Pine Labs also says issuers allow only one Reserve Pay mandate per user, and lists Google Pay and PhonePe as "Coming Soon" ([Pine Labs FAQ](https://www.pinelabs.com/docs/online-payments/upi-reserve-pay/faqs)). Setu describes a UPI block of up to ₹1 lakh for "conditional payments" ([Setu docs](https://docs.setu.co/payments/umap/mandates/reserve)). That conflicts with the ₹10,000 cap and could not be reconciled against a primary NPCI source. Real villa prices show why the cap matters. A two-night stay at StayVista's Karjat villa, which sleeps 14, lists at ₹65,258 a night plus ₹15,811 in taxes and fees ([MakeMyTrip](https://www.makemytrip.com/hotels/stayvista_at_the_den-details-karjat.html)). That is about **₹11,600 per head even split 14 ways** and over ₹20,000 per head for eight, above the cap in both cases. UPI Circle does the opposite of pooling: one primary user lets up to five others spend from their account ([NPCI](https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/press-releases/2024/NPCI-Press-Release-RBI-Governor-Launches-Key-Digital-Payment-Initiatives-at-Global-Fintech-Festival-2024.pdf)).

### Existing split apps leave conditional commitment open

Existing products track who owes what and settle afterwards. None of them makes payment conditional on enough people committing. Google Pay's split feature sends requests to up to 20 people, but "doesn't have a mechanism to force someone to pay" ([Razorpay Learn](https://razorpay.com/learn/how-to-split-bills-in-gpay/)). Paytm's Split Bills pre-fills a UPI payment or lets members mark payments made elsewhere ([ok.com](https://us.ok.com/ask_news_cars/paytm-split-bills-group-payment-feature-launched-for-shared-expenses/)). tricount has "No in-app UPI transfers" ([tricount blog](https://tricount.com/blog/top-splitwise-alternatives-in-india-2025-which-app-should-you-switch-to)). Conditional commitment is the gap in the market, and the regulated rails are exactly why it is still open.

The four ways to build "money moves only if enough commit" compare as follows:

| Option | Who holds the money before quorum | Financially binding? | Cost on a ₹10,000 share | Feasible now? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Pledge in app, then UPI link to organiser | Nobody; money stays with each friend | No; social pressure only | ₹0 | Yes; no licence, any UPI app |
| B. Organiser sends gateway payment links after quorum | Gateway, settling to the organiser | Yes once paid; refunds go to source | ~₹236 at 2% + 18% GST after launch offers | Yes, but the organiser does merchant KYC, and whether pooling counts as acceptable use is unclear |
| C. App sells the stay as merchant of record, auto-refunds if quorum fails | Gateway, settling to the app | Yes | ~₹236, plus ~₹40 if the 0.4% fee applies | Needs a company, GST registration and travel-seller obligations |
| D. Reserve Pay block in each friend's account | Each friend's bank, blocked | Yes | Gateway pricing | No: ₹10,000 cap, no lodging category, patchy app support |

The fee figures come from Razorpay's standard 2% UPI platform fee plus 18% GST ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-0-percent-platform-fee-offer-90-days-new-merchants-2026/)) and NPCI's proposed 0.4% fee on merchant payments above ₹2,000, which has been deferred to "immediately post Diwali" ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/upi-mdr-charges-wont-kick-in-before-diwali-npci-defers-rollout)). The MVP should be Option A. Friends pledge in the app before a deadline. Once enough have pledged, each friend gets a `upi://pay` link to the organiser with a fixed amount and a trip reference, and the organiser confirms receipts.

The villa's own free-cancellation window can then act as the hold that UPI cannot provide. The organiser books a refundable rate, collects shares while full refunds are still possible, and cancels without penalty if friends don't pay. The cost of this design is that the organiser fronts the money and carries the risk that someone doesn't pay. The product's job is to make that risk visible and short-lived. Option C is the path to a truly binding commitment once there is enough volume to justify becoming a travel seller. One legal question remains open. No ruling was found on whether an app that only records pledges and generates P2P links counts as "operating a payment system". Because it never handles funds, it appears not to, but this is unverified.

## A 14-day refund cliff sets the commitment deadline

Branded Indian villas follow a consistent three-step refund schedule. StayVista's published terms refund **100% at 14 or more days before check-in, 50% between 14 and 7 days, and nothing within 7 days**, with 18% GST added ([StayVista T&C](https://www.americanexpress.com/content/dam/amex/in/business/saving-pass/StayVista_TC1.pdf)). Those terms come from a 2023 offer. StayVista's 2026 MakeMyTrip listings show the same shape: "Free Cancellation till 02 Dec", "50% refundable if cancelled before 09 Dec", then no refund ([MakeMyTrip: The Den](https://www.makemytrip.com/hotels/stayvista_at_the_den-details-karjat.html)). Lohono's Goa listing does the same, with a free-cancellation date followed by a 50% tier exactly 7 days later ([MakeMyTrip: Lohono](https://www.makemytrip.com/hotels/lohonostays_estate_de_alta_palmeira-details-goa.html)). The check-in dates weren't visible in the fetched pages, so the exact day counts on those listings are inferred from the matching gaps.

Airbnb hosts choose from standard policies. Flexible refunds in full until 24 hours before check-in and Moderate until 5 days before. **Limited, introduced for bookings from 1 October 2025, refunds in full until 14 days before and 50% between 14 and 7 days**, the same shape as StayVista. Firm refunds in full until 30 days before and 50% between 30 and 7 days. Every policy includes a 24-hour full-refund grace period if the booking is made at least 7 days before check-in ([Airbnb Help 475](https://www.airbnb.co.in/help/article/475)). One rule matters a lot for Indian groups: Airbnb's "Pay Part Now, Part Later" and "Reserve Now, Pay Later" are **not available when the guest pays in Indian rupees** ([Airbnb Help 2143](https://www.airbnb.co.in/help/article/2143)). The friend who books an Airbnb therefore fronts the whole stay on the day they book.

Independent homestays set their own terms. A Kerala booking site confirms stays only after "advance or full payment as per the property's policy", with no refund for no-shows ([munnar.com](https://munnar.com/terms)). A villa operator in Maharashtra refunds 75% at 8 or more days, 50% at 4–7 days and nothing under 3 days ([Treks and Trails](https://treksandtrails.org/tours/treks-and-trails-india-malavli-villa)). The widely assumed "50% advance" norm for direct homestay bookings could not be confirmed from any authoritative source.

These schedules imply two deadlines, not one. The deadline that matters is the free-cancellation cut-off, because after it every friend who drops out costs the group 50–100% of their share. Collecting money takes time, because payment confirmation under Option A is manual. So the pledge deadline should close several days before the cut-off. That means about **17–21 days before check-in for StayVista, Lohono and Airbnb Limited stays, and about 33–37 days for Airbnb Firm**. The 3–7-day buffer is a design judgement, not a sourced norm. Flexible and Moderate Airbnb listings allow much later deadlines and suit spontaneous trips. If the app ever gets Booking.com Demand API access, it can set the deadline automatically: availability responses include `policies.cancellation.free_cancellation_until` and a full refund schedule ([Booking.com Developers](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/accommodation-tutorial)). House rules belong in the pledge screen as well. Lohono's Goa villa states that **"Groups with only male guests are not allowed"**, and StayVista's Alibaug villa bars unmarried couples and outside food ([MakeMyTrip: Lohono](https://www.makemytrip.com/hotels/lohonostays_estate_de_alta_palmeira-details-goa.html); [MakeMyTrip: Amara](https://www.makemytrip.com/hotels/stayvista_amara_villa-details-alibaug.html)). None of the APIs reviewed exposes these rules as structured data. A friend group that discovers them after paying faces exactly the failure the app exists to prevent.

## Stay and train supply arrives as links, not inventory

Airbnb is closed to small partners. Its open affiliate programme ended on **31 March 2021**, and members were told to remove all links ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/01/airbnb-ends-affiliate-program-as-it-ramps-up-host-recruitment-campaign.html)). Its official API is by invitation only and serves software for managing listings, not guest search ([Apify](https://blog.apify.com/what-you-should-know-about-the-airbnb-api-and-free-alternatives/)). Airbnb does not document its search URL parameters. The most-used open-source Airbnb tool warns that it breaks whenever Airbnb changes its page structure and tells users to respect Airbnb's terms ([openbnb MCP server](https://github.com/openbnb-org/mcp-server-airbnb)). For Airbnb, the realistic integration is a "paste the listing link" field, with price, dates and cancellation policy entered by a person.

Booking.com has two tiers. Since **20 June 2025** its affiliate programme runs through Awin ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/booking-com-moves-all-affiliates-to-awin-what-to-know-about-termination-notices)). The programme accepts any "promotional property", apps included, with no stated traffic threshold, and provides deep links and search widgets that redirect to Booking.com ([Mize](https://mize.tech/blog/all-about-the-booking-com-affiliate-partner-program-for-travel-agents/)). The Demand API is the version with structured data. It requires **"Registration as a Booking.com Managed Affiliate Partner"** and Partner Centre access granted by an account manager after a signed contract ([Booking.com Developers](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites)). Booking inside the app ("search-look-book") additionally "requires approval, a sound business case, and an appropriate partner agreement". Partners without it must send users to the returned URL ([Booking.com Developers](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/accommodation-tutorial)). Booking.com does not document its public search-results URL format either. The earliest community record uses fields that have since changed ([GitHub gist, 2012](https://gist.github.com/agustinhaller/4132659)), so the URL the API returns is the only stable deep link. Whether Booking.com carries meaningful Indian villa and homestay inventory was not verified, and that limits how useful it is.

Agoda is the friendliest to developers. Its Affiliate Lite API takes a city or hotel list, dates and occupancy, and returns the daily rate, discount and a landing URL, authenticated with a site ID and API key. It returns **no cancellation-policy fields** ([Agoda API PDF](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/Documents/AffiliateLiteApi/Affiliate_Lite_API_V2.0.pdf)). Online affiliates get search only. Booking and post-booking need Agoda certification, a finance account and a signed contract ([Agoda Developer](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs)). One developer guide rates Agoda the most open to individuals ([dev.to](https://dev.to/kouta222/complete-guide-for-individual-developers-recommended-hotel-affiliate-apis-by-region-4c1e)).

The Indian supply that friend groups actually book is the hardest to integrate. MakeMyTrip publishes no developer portal. Its affiliate access runs through Cuelinks, where the campaign was **"paused by the advertiser"** when checked ([Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/makemytrip-holidays-india-affiliate-program)). Its myPartner platform is aimed at offline travel agents ([TravelTrendsToday](https://www.traveltrendstoday.in/makemytrip-launches-mypartner-to-empower-travel-agents)). StayVista and Lohono sell through MakeMyTrip, so an MMT deep link is the indirect route to branded villas. Neither brand has a public affiliate or API programme. No reliable current information turned up on "Vista Rooms", which may be a confusion with StayVista.

Transport follows the same pattern. IRCTC told the Delhi High Court it is the sole authorised e-ticket seller. In 2019 the court ruled that RailYatri, which had no contract with IRCTC, was **"neither a PSP nor an RSP"**, meaning neither a licensed principal nor a retail service provider ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2019/04/223-railyatri-applies-for-licence-to-sell-train-tickets-after-defeat-in-high-court/)). ixigo is an "IRCTC Authorised Partner", but even there users "Log in using your IRCTC user ID and password" ([ixigo](https://ixigo.com/trains)). In practice each friend books their own ticket, and the app records the PNR. Affiliate programmes for redBus and ixigo exist on EarnKaro and Cuelinks. They were paused when checked, and redBus lists deep linking as "NA" ([EarnKaro: redBus](https://earnkaro.com/blog/redbus-affiliate-program/); [Cuelinks: ixigo](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/ixigo-affiliate-program)). No documented deep-link formats exist for IRCTC, ixigo or redBus, so links can only point to their home or search pages. For a road trip, a Google Maps directions URL with waypoints (see the next section) is enough.

Real-time booking status is available only at booking-level tiers: Booking.com's Orders and Notifications services, and Agoda's Post-Booking API for fulfilment partners ([Booking.com Developers](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/overview); [Agoda Developer](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs)). The low-risk substitute is a forwarding address unique to each trip. The person who booked forwards their confirmation email, and the app extracts the dates, amount and cancellation deadline. AwardWallet sells an email-parsing API that extracts cancellation deadlines. API keys are issued on request, and it does not say it supports MakeMyTrip, IRCTC or redBus ([AwardWallet](https://awardwallet.com/email-parsing-api)). Reading users' inboxes directly is far heavier. The `gmail.readonly` scope is **restricted**, and storing that data on servers triggers a security assessment ([Gmail API scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes)). One vendor puts the annual cost at roughly US$500–4,500 ([DeepStrike](https://deepstrike.io/blog/google-casa-security-assessment-2025)). Forwarded confirmations contain personal data, so India's DPDP Rules apply. They were notified on **13 November 2025**, and the consent-notice and erasure duties take effect 18 months later ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified)), which falls around May 2027. The app should store only the parsed fields and delete raw emails.

## WhatsApp, maps and calendars cost nothing; groups and albums stay manual

WhatsApp is the main coordination channel, and the free path is the right one. The official click-to-chat format `https://wa.me/?text=<urlencoded>` opens WhatsApp with a pre-filled message and lets the user pick any chat or group ([WhatsApp FAQ](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/general/26000030)). That covers "share this poll", "pledge deadline in 2 days" and "here's what you owe", with no credentials and no cost. No official API lets a third-party app post into a user's existing private group. Meta's Groups API lets only an **Official Business Account** create *new*, invite-only groups ([Meta Groups API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/groups/get-started/)). One vendor reports a cap of 8 participants per group ([Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/whatsapp-group-api/)), which is unverified and would be too small for many trips anyway. Sending 1:1 reminders through the Cloud API has become more expensive. Pricing has been per delivered message since 1 July 2025. **From 1 October 2026, service messages and in-window utility templates are charged again**, with 1,000 free service messages per number per month. Indian accounts must move to INR billing by 31 December 2026 or stop delivering messages from 1 January 2027 ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). Since 15 January 2026, Meta's terms have also barred businesses whose *primary* function is AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform)), which matters if the product is built around an AI trip planner. The group's own invite link (`chat.whatsapp.com/…`), pasted into the trip by a friend, is enough.

Calendars and maps are solved by open standards and documented URLs. An `.ics` file under RFC 5545 works across Google, Apple and Outlook and can be dropped into a WhatsApp chat ([RFC 5545](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545)). Google's `render?action=TEMPLATE` link gives a one-tap add, but its parameters were **"recovered by reading the Google Calendar web client's JavaScript"**, so Google could change them without notice ([IDF docs](https://github.com/InteractionDesignFoundation/add-event-to-calendar-docs/blob/master/services/google.md)). Writing events through the Calendar API needs OAuth scopes such as `calendar.events.owned` and, for a public app, Google's verification process ([Calendar auth](https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/auth)). An MVP does not need any of that. Google states plainly: **"You don't need a Google API key to use Maps URLs."** Those URLs cover place search and directions with up to 9 waypoints (3 in mobile browsers), including a `two-wheeler` travel mode where it is available ([Maps URLs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started)). Places autocomplete would need a billing account. Since 1 March 2025 Google has given free monthly allowances per SKU instead of the old US$200 credit, and India gets larger allowances: Google's worked example implies 70,000 free geocoding requests a month ([Maps pricing changes](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/march-2025)).

Weather is free with one catch. Open-Meteo needs no key and allows 10,000 calls a day, but its free tier excludes **"Websites or apps with subscriptions or advertisements"**, and its data requires CC BY 4.0 attribution ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)). OpenWeatherMap's free key allows 60 calls a minute and 1 million a month ([OWM pricing](https://openweathermap.org/full-price)), which makes it the fallback once the app earns money. IMD is the authoritative source for monsoon and cyclone warnings. Access requires either JWT onboarding on its portal or an IP-whitelisting request approved by email, and it publishes no pricing or terms ([IMD portal](https://api.imd.gov.in/); [IMD whitelisting form](https://city.imd.gov.in/citywx/api_request.php)). Linking out to IMD's Mausam service is enough for warnings.

Photos and expenses complete the picture. Since **31 March 2025** the Google Photos Library API only touches content the app created itself, and the sharing endpoints (`albums.share`, `sharedAlbums.join`, `sharedAlbums.list`) return 403 errors. Google tells developers to send users to the Photos app to share ([Photos API updates](https://developers.google.com/photos/support/updates)). A shared trip album is therefore a link that one friend pastes in. Splitwise offers a self-serve OAuth API, but warns that its "conservative rate and access limits" are "not well suited to commercial projects" and directs commercial integrators to email the company ([Splitwise api-docs mirror](https://ohotnik.duckdns.org/splitwise/api-docs/blob/main/splitwise.yaml)). That text comes from a third-party mirror, because the canonical page was blocked. Splitwise's free tier limits users to 3–4 expenses a day, with Pro at ₹149 a month ([ITVoice](https://www.itvoice.in/splitwise-has-introduced-restrictions-on-the-number-of-free-expenses-users-can-add)). A simple ledger built into the app, settled with the same `upi://pay` links used for the advance, is better than integrating with Splitwise.

## Affiliate revenue is thin, and gateway fees are a cost

Booking.com's affiliate programme is the only open, tiered and live commission programme found. It pays **25% to 40% of Booking.com's own commission**, rising with stayed reservations a month: 25% for up to 50, 30% for 51–150, 35% for 151–500 and 40% above 500. Mize's illustrative example assumes Booking.com earns 15% on a booking, which makes the entry tier worth about 3.75% of booking value ([Mize](https://mize.tech/blog/all-about-the-booking-com-affiliate-partner-program-for-travel-agents/)). Agoda's reward through Travelpayouts was 6% of reservation value excluding taxes, but that figure dates from 2019–2023 and may be stale ([Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/3068)). Everything else pays little or nothing. Airbnb pays affiliates no cash ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/01/airbnb-ends-affiliate-program-as-it-ramps-up-host-recruitment-campaign.html)). MakeMyTrip pays up to 1.89% per sale and ₹27 per iOS install, but the campaign was paused ([Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/makemytrip-holidays-india-affiliate-program)). ixigo listed up to 2.52% on hotels and ₹202.50 per flight, also paused ([Cuelinks: ixigo](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/ixigo-affiliate-program)). redBus pays up to ₹150 per booking, paused, with no deep linking ([EarnKaro: redBus](https://earnkaro.com/blog/redbus-affiliate-program/)).

The StayVista Karjat stay shows how small the numbers are. Two nights cost about ₹1.3 lakh before taxes. At MakeMyTrip's best rate, and only if the campaign resumes, that earns about **₹2,500**. Booked on Booking.com at the entry tier under Mize's 15% assumption, the same value would earn about ₹4,900, but there is no evidence that StayVista lists on Booking.com. The inventory Indian friend groups most often book (branded villas on MakeMyTrip, Airbnb homes and independent homestays booked directly) pays the least. A booking-commission model would also push the product toward whichever platform pays, rather than whichever villa suits the group.

"Merchant fees" are not realistic income for an app that stays out of the money flow. They become a cost the moment anyone collects through a gateway. Razorpay charges a **2% platform fee on UPI plus 18% GST** and a ₹199 KYC fee. It waives the platform fee for 90 days or ₹5 lakh for accounts activated from 1 July 2026 ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-0-percent-platform-fee-offer-90-days-new-merchants-2026/)). Cashfree's standard fee is 1.95%, waived on up to ₹20 lakh of volume for sign-ups until 31 March 2027 ([Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/payment-gateway-charges)). PhonePe PG shows its 1.99% fee struck through as "Free*" ([PhonePe PG](https://www.phonepe.com/business-solutions/payment-gateway/pricing/)). Collecting the full ₹1.62 lakh for the Karjat stay at standard Razorpay rates would cost about **₹3,800, more than the best-case MakeMyTrip commission on the same booking**. A new UPI merchant fee of 0.4% above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300, is due after Diwali, and P2P transfers stay exempt ([Khaleej Times / ANI](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/india-defers-upi-charges-on-some-merchant-transactions-to-after-diwali)). Parliament cleared the legal path for such fees in August 2026 ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/parliament-passes-taxation-laws-amendment-bill-paving-way-for-possible-upi-charges-545218)). The app earns on payments only as merchant of record (Option C), where it sets its own margin on the stay. Margin economics for that route were not researched. Monetising in any form also raises costs: Open-Meteo moves to a paid plan, WhatsApp utility messages now cost money, and commercial use of Splitwise needs written approval.

## Conclusion

The regulation that blocks the obvious design also protects the opportunity. "Pool the money, release it at quorum" is closed to anyone without ₹15 crore of net worth, a PPI partner or a merchant-of-record business, and the collect ban removed the native pull request. So no incumbent offers conditional commitment, and a newcomer can only compete on behaviour and design, not on payment plumbing. The most useful idea here is that the villa's free-cancellation window can do the escrow's job. If the app treats the 14-day or 30-day cut-off as the real deadline and works backwards to a pledge deadline, it delivers most of the value of an all-or-nothing escrow without touching a rupee. The organiser carries a short, visible exposure instead of an open-ended one.

The economics point away from booking commissions as the business model. The inventory Indian groups actually book pays little or nothing to affiliates. Collecting money through a gateway costs more than the best commission, and monetising turns several free utilities into paid ones. Four dates could change these classifications: NPCI widening Reserve Pay to lodging categories, raising its ₹10,000 cap, or Google Pay and PhonePe adding bank-account support; the post-Diwali merchant-fee rollout; WhatsApp's INR-billing cut-off on 1 January 2027; and the DPDP duties that take effect around May 2027. Until one of them changes, the strongest MVP collects pledges in the app, moves money directly between friends, and keeps every booking as a link.
